Calculating Landed Cost & Profit Margin on Liquidation Inventory

📅 Updated September 2026 ⏱️ 8 Min Read ✍️ Simpatico Advisory Desk

Profitable wholesale liquidation reselling depends on disciplined financial modeling. Looking strictly at the purchase price of a pallet without accounting for freight delivery, testing labor, marketplace seller fees, and shrink allowances leads to distorted profit projections.

Financial Breakdown:
  • 1. The Landed Cost Formula
  • 2. Accounting for Shrink & Salvage Loss
  • 3. Marketplace Fees & Labor Overhead
  • 4. Step-by-Step Profitability Case Study
  • 5. Interactive Calculation Tool

1. The Landed Cost Formula

Landed Cost is the total cost of bringing a liquidation unit into your warehouse, ready for sale to an end consumer:

Total Landed Cost = Wholesale Invoice Price + Freight Shipping + Liftgate Surcharges + Receiving Labor

2. Accounting for Shrink & Salvage Loss

In wholesale returns, a percentage of items will be damaged, incomplete, or unsellable. Factoring in shrink protects your net margin:

  • Overstock / Case Pack: 1% – 3% estimated shrink
  • Store Shelf Pulls: 5% – 10% estimated shrink
  • Customer Returns: 15% – 25% estimated shrink
💰 Profit Margin Formula

Net Profit Margin (%) = [(Total Gross Realized Sales - Total Landed Cost - Selling Fees) / Total Gross Realized Sales] × 100

4. Step-by-Step Profitability Case Study

Consider a typical 2-pallet order of consumer electronics:

  • Pallet Invoice Cost: $1,200 (100 manifested items)
  • Freight Cost: $300 (including residential liftgate fee)
  • Total Initial Outlay: $1,500 ($15.00 per unit initial landed cost)
  • Shrink Rate (20%): 20 non-working items written off; 80 units sold.
  • Average Resale Price per Working Item: $45.00
  • Gross Sales Realized (80 units × $45): $3,600.00
  • Platform Selling & Processing Fees (15%): -$540.00
  • Net Realized Cash ($3,600 - $540): $3,060.00
  • Net Profit ($3,060 - $1,500 Landed Outlay): +$1,560.00 (+104% ROI)
