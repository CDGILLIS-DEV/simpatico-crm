How to Read & Evaluate Wholesale Liquidation Manifests

📅 Updated September 2026 ⏱️ 7 Min Read ✍️ Simpatico Audit Desk

A liquidation manifest is an itemized inventory document generated by retailers or liquidators detailing the contents of a pallet or truckload. Learning how to analyze a manifest allows buyers to calculate exact cost-per-unit, estimate secondary market resale values, and spot unmanifested risks before placing an order.

Guide Checklist:
  • 1. Core Fields in a Wholesale Manifest
  • 2. MSRP vs. Actual Secondary Market Value
  • 3. Factoring in Shrink & Damage Rates
  • 4. Fully Manifested vs. Unmanifested Lots
  • 5. Next Steps for Buyers

1. Core Fields in a Wholesale Manifest

A comprehensive manifest includes the following standardized data points:

  • UPC / EAN Barcode: The 12-digit universal product code used to cross-reference Amazon, eBay, and Google Shopping price history.
  • Item Description & Brand: Detailed product title, model number, and manufacturer.
  • Quantity (Qty): Total number of units included for each line item.
  • Unit MSRP: Original retail selling price assigned by the retailer.
  • Extended MSRP: Total retail value for the line item (Unit MSRP × Quantity).
  • Condition Grade: Specified lot condition (Overstock, Customer Return, Damaged Packaging).

2. MSRP vs. Actual Secondary Market Value

A common mistake among novice buyers is assuming items will sell at 100% of original MSRP. In secondary liquidation markets:

  • Overstock & NIB: Sells for 50% to 75% of original MSRP on eBay or Amazon.
  • Open-Box / Tested Working: Sells for 35% to 50% of MSRP.
  • Customer Return (As-Is): Sells for 20% to 35% of MSRP.
📊 Valuation Formula

To estimate your realistic gross revenue, multiply the Total Manifest MSRP by 40% (0.40). Submitting bids or evaluating lots based on realistic secondary value protects your gross margin.

3. Factoring in Shrink & Damage Rates

When purchasing customer return lots, account for a standard 15% to 25% shrink factor representing non-repairable, incomplete, or unsellable units. Incorporate shrink into your landed cost calculation using our Landed Cost Calculator.

4. Fully Manifested vs. Unmanifested Lots

  • Fully Manifested: Itemized SKU breakdown provided. Offers low risk and predictable profit modeling.
  • Unmanifested General Merchandise: Sold by pallet count or weight without individual UPC listings. Offers lower purchase prices per unit but higher category variance.
